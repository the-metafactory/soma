# Experience first

**When it applies:** a product, UX or feature-scope tradeoff.

**Rule:** when implementation convenience conflicts with the experience of whoever consumes the work, choose the experience.

- Justify every feature, control and option. Three polished features beat ten rough ones.
- Prototype the decision cheaply before building it for real.
- Get the details right: transitions, alignment, spacing, feedback, error states.
- Every feature serves the core loop or gets out of its way.
- The consumer may be an end user, a developer importing a library, or the engineer who maintains the code next. Explain impact from their side.

Foundations serve the experience. `references/principles/foundational-thinking.md` orders the work; this sets its target.
